Your assessment week

This is how I'd run your first week. It shows you exactly what you're eating and how your calories affect your weight. This is real data, not a guess.

Eat like you normally do for 7 days. Don't clean it up because you're tracking. Weigh in every morning after the toilet, before food, and log your calories, protein, carbs and fat for everything you eat. Oils, drinks and the bits you pick at all count. Then fill in About you at the bottom so you can compare your week against the calculator.

If you don't want to do this, it's optional. You can just use the calorie calculator further down. It just might not be as accurate.

Are you ready to bulk?

The best bulks start lean. If you're already carrying too much fat, a bulk just adds more on top, you'll feel soft within a month and you'll end up cutting for twice as long.

You need to be honest with yourself. Are there some visible abs there? You don't really want to be much over 12% body fat if you want a LONG, productive bulk.

The leaner the better, so if you aren't there yet, get lean first and then start.

Your week vs the calculator

Most lads have only ever seen the calculator number. Here's what your body actually did.

Pick your split

Pick the one you'll still be doing in six months. A 3 day split you hit every week beats a 5 day split you hit twice. Then log every set. Grey numbers are what you did last week. Beat them.

Make sure you're training hard and pushing your sets as close to failure, or to failure, as you can. When you're bulking it's a time to get strong, so make sure you're making progress.

How I'd run it

The goal isn't to get the scale up as quickly as possible. It's to stay in the bulk for as long as you can. The longer you're in it, the better.

1

Start lean

The best bulks are done from being lean. Starting lean means you can stay in the surplus for months before you get soft, and that time is what builds muscle. If you're already carrying too much fat, cut first. Bulking season will still be here.

2

Start small, let the data decide

Your starting surplus is a guess. Your weekly weigh-ins are the truth. Start somewhere sensible, then adjust off what the scale actually does. Some lads end up 500 or more over where they started because that's what their body needs to grow. That's fine. What you don't do is jump straight into a massive surplus because it feels productive. Three weeks later you're soft, your jeans are tight and you bin it.

3

Weigh daily, judge weekly

Daily weight jumps around with salt, carbs and sleep. Take the weekly average and compare it to last week's. That's the only number that matters.

4

Adjust every 2 weeks, not every day

Gaining faster than your target range? Drop 100 calories. Not moving after 2 weeks? Add 100–200. Small moves, then give it time.

When you add calories, put them into carbs. That's what fuels your training. If your fats started on the low side, bring them up a bit first. Further into the bulk, when eating more starts to feel like a job, add some of the calories as fats instead. They're easy calories and a lot easier to get down.

5

Protein builds it

Pick your number between 1.7 and 2.2g per kg and hit it every day, training or not. In a bulk you're already in a surplus, so you don't need to force it sky high. I sit nearer the lower end and put those calories into carbs.

6

Carbs fuel it

Carbs are what make your sessions feel strong. More carbs, more reps, better recovery, more growth. They take up whatever calories are left after protein and fat, and if you split training and rest days, they're the one macro that moves: a bit more on training days, a bit less on rest days.

7

Fats keep you running

Fats look after your hormones, joints and energy. Keep them between 0.6 and 1.1g per kg. Lower gives you more room for carbs, higher if that's how you like to eat. Get them from real food: eggs, red meat, olive oil, nuts, oily fish.

8

Steps and cardio stay in

Bulking isn't permission to stop moving. Keep your daily steps where they were in your assessment week. Drop them and your maintenance drops with them, so the surplus gets bigger without you noticing.

Keep some cardio in too. Two or three easy sessions a week of 20 to 30 minutes, like an incline walk, bike or a steady run, is plenty. It keeps your fitness and appetite up, helps you recover between sessions, and makes the cut at the end a lot less painful.

Whatever you're doing in your assessment week, keep doing it. If you add more cardio mid-bulk, add a bit of food to cover it.

9

Know when to call it

If you've got too soft and you've started to feel like shit, you might need a small diet break for 2–4 weeks to reset, then go again. Or if it's time to cut for summer, then do it.
